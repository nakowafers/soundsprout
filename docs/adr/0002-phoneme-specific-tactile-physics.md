# Phoneme-Specific Tactile Physics on Letter Cards

`Letter Card` interactions use distinct gesture mechanics depending on whether the target grapheme represents a **Continuous Phoneme** or a **Stop Phoneme**:

- **Continuous Phonemes** (`/ă/`, `/m/`, `/s/`, `/f/`, `/ĭ/`, `/n/`): Practiced via a **press-and-hold** gesture that sustains the phoneme audio and continuously grows the sprout while held.
- **Stop Phonemes** (`/t/`, `/p/`): Practiced via **3 crisp tap-pops** of seed pods that play an instantaneous, unvoiced plosive burst on each tap.

## Context & Trade-off

A uniform press-and-hold mechanic across all letters would force stop consonants (`/t/`, `/p/`) to sustain sound over time, inevitably introducing a trailing schwa vowel ("tuhhh", "puhhh") that corrupts subsequent CVC blending ("tuh-a-puh" instead of "tap"). Conversely, reducing all letters to quick single taps would forfeit the tactile sensation of stretching continuous sounds (`/sss/`, `/mmm/`), which is the kinesthetic foundation for UFLI's continuous blending routine. Differentiating the gesture physics by phoneme class aligns the child's physical finger movement directly with the acoustic reality of the phoneme.
